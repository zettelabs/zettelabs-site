Simple Alarm page: calm by design, with a sea that never stops

This audit covers the Simple Alarm app page (/simple-alarm) after its redesign as the app's own alarm cards: the hero rise, the folder tabs, the screens index and plate, the scroll reveals, the looping waves video and the live Edit Alarm dialog. It is read through three motion lenses, weighted for an alarm utility.

Overall

The page already moves like a good alarm app: transform and opacity only, short state changes, a live countdown that updates instantly, and a sunrise that stays still. Four things let it down. The waves video plays from first load, even while it is far off screen, and nobody can stop it. The folder tabs come apart from their folders on hover and stay lifted after a tap. The screens plate dims while one screenshot crossfades into the next. And the shared reveal runs on thirteen elements, six of them plain headings and paragraphs.

ImportantEmilJhey

The waves loop forever, off screen, with no way to stop them

WhatThe script calls video.play() on load. Measured in the browser at the top of the page, the video is already playing (paused: false, fully buffered) while the "A morning" section is several screens below. It never pauses, and there is no control to stop it.

Why it mattersMoving content that runs longer than five seconds next to other content needs a way to pause it (WCAG 2.2.2). Reduced motion covers only visitors who set it in their OS. And a video playing out of sight costs phone data and battery for nothing.

Recommended motionPlay only while the section is on screen (IntersectionObserver), and add a small "Pause the waves" toggle (aria-pressed, the same pattern as the site's marquee toggle) in the section's corner. Under reduced motion it starts paused, and the toggle can still play it.

ImportantEmilJakub

Folder tabs come apart from their folders, and stay lifted after a tap

What.sa-tab:hover moves the tab up 4px. The tab ends exactly at its band's top edge, so the lift opens a 4px seam (measured: tab bottom 404px, band top 408px) through which the band behind, or the sunrise, shows. The rule is plain :hover, so on a phone the tab stays lifted after a tap until something else is touched.

Why it mattersThe idea of the hero is that each tab is its folder. A tab that floats free breaks that object for a moment, on the one hover the page invites. Sticky hover on touch reads as a stuck state.

Recommended motionKeep the 4px / 200ms lift, but give the tab a 4px foot that runs down into its own band (same colour, hidden at rest), so the lift reveals more tab instead of a gap. Limit the lift to @media (hover: hover).

ImportantJakubEmil

The screens plate dims mid-crossfade, and the index trails the pointer

WhatSwitching rows fades the old screenshot out and the new one in at the same time, both 240ms ease-out. Halfway through, each is near half opacity, so the dark plate shows through and the phone screen dims for a beat. The index row highlight also runs at 240ms while the pointer sweeps the list, so the grey bar lags a row behind.

Why it mattersA crossfade should feel like one screen turning into another, not like the lights flickering. The index is the most-touched control on the page; at pointer speed it should feel attached to the pointer.

Recommended motionFade the incoming screenshot in on top (200ms, the page curve) while the outgoing one stays fully opaque underneath, then drop it once covered. Shorten the index highlight to 150ms. Keep the instant swap under reduced motion.

ImportantJakubEmil

Thirteen identical reveals, six of them on plain text

Whatdata-reveal sits on 13 elements: the h2 and paragraph of Screens, A morning and Set one (6), plus the plate, index list, button cells, settings sheet, settings screenshot, slip and dialog. All rise the same 12px over 400ms. The site-wide audit took reveals off headings and body text; this page adds them back.

Why it mattersThis is the uniform-fade and motion-on-static-text pattern. When every block rises, none of them leads, and the visitor waits to read words that could have been there. In the Screens section, the plate, index and cells arrive together and cascade, which is a lot of motion for one panel.

Recommended motionOne object per section rises: the plate, the settings sheet, the settings screenshot, the slip and the dialog. Headings, paragraphs, the index list and the button cells are simply there.

Emil Kowalski — Restraint & Speed

An alarm page should feel calm and dependable, and most of its motion is already restrained. The real gaps were about control (a loop with no stop, a hover that sticks) and about motion running where nobody asked for it. Jakub finished the details people touch; Jhey only confirmed the delight is in the right two places.

  • Lean EmilDrop the scroll reveals on this page entirely and start the waves paused. Nothing moves until the visitor does.
  • Lean JakubKeep one object rising per section at 8–12px and give the tab click a motion-safe smooth scroll.
  • Lean JheyBuild the printing slip, and let the sea ripple under the tab stack on load once.
